# Nominal versus real income

The user requested the finished video and explicitly delegated every editorial
and production decision. The selected direction, script approval, and final
export are within that delegation. The user subsequently requested vertical
4K at 60 fps: the current export is 2160×3840 at 60 fps.

## 4K / 60 fps revision

Render the original SVG and text composition directly at the larger output
resolution. The 1080×1920 design coordinate system scales uniformly by 2,
including captions. Animation and caption clocks remain in seconds, preserving
playback speed. Frame-rounded scene boundaries use the configured 60 fps;
the music generator and review-frame extractor use the same rate. Narration WAVs
and their predicted word timings are reused unchanged. Encoding settings remain
H.264 CRF 18 and the existing JPEG intermediate-frame settings.

The original named `output/nominal-vs-real-income.mp4` remains the 1080p30 copy.
Its metadata and source snapshot are in `output/archive-1080p30/`. The new
delivery copy is `output/nominal-vs-real-income-4k60.mp4`; the workflow's canonical
`output/final.mp4` becomes the new version after final checks pass.

## Content and art direction

One hypothetical example: monthly pay $3,000 → $3,150 (+5%), while the same
price basket rises $100 → $108 (+8%) over the same year. Real monthly income
in last year's dollars is $3,150 / 1.08 ≈ $2,917. Exact real-income change is
(1.05 / 1.08 − 1) × 100 ≈ −2.8%. The charts use a common zero baseline and
last-year index of 100; the small difference in bar heights is intentional.
These figures are illustrative, not current economic statistics.

Warm paper, dark ink, teal for nominal pay, orange for inflation and the real
income loss. Large serif headlines, sans-serif data, original vector drawings.
The sequence moves from opposing pay/buying-power arrows to a changing pay slip,
an unchanged basket with a rising price, the adjustment formula, diverging
indexed bars, and money moving into a basket. Key copy occupies x=90–900 and
y=150–1250. Captions occupy y=1330–1500, with a secondary source footer at1570.

Scene durations use measured narration plus 0.45 seconds of tail, rounded up to
frames. All scene motion is driven by frame time and timed to the generated
voice. Captions use actual Kokoro-predicted word times through the shared
`KaraokeCaptions` component; no equal-duration word estimation.

## Sound

Original 100 BPM mallet/bass groove generated by `compose_music.py`, with no
external samples. The hook has an inquisitive motif; explanations use fewer
notes; the reveal increases motif density; the ending resolves to a C-major
chord and fades out. No separate SFX are needed.

Voice: local Kokoro `af_heart`, American English, speed1.0, 24 kHz. Model license
and asset provenance are in `assets/sources.json`. Music includes word-interval
ducking, musical arc and fades, so renderer gain is1 and renderer fades are0.
`output/mix-measurements.json` compares voice and music over the same voiced
intervals after these operations. The overall RMS difference is15 dB; scene
differences range from about13.7 to16.0 dB. These measurements do not certify
subjective audibility or emotional fit. Listening, pronunciation by ear and
physical phone playback are not available in this tool session and must remain
`not-performed` in the QC record. Technical checks and rendered-frame inspection
are recorded separately.

## Reproduce

From the workspace root, with the repository's existing Node24.21.0 environment
loaded and `.venv-kokoro` available:

```bash
HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 .venv-kokoro/bin/python tools/kokoro/generate_audio.py --project projects/005-nominal-vs-real-income
.venv-kokoro/bin/python projects/005-nominal-vs-real-income/compose_music.py
npm run video -- preflight --project projects/005-nominal-vs-real-income
npm run video -- render --project projects/005-nominal-vs-real-income --preview
# Inspect the preview, contact sheet, motion and karaoke before final export.
npm run video -- render --project projects/005-nominal-vs-real-income --final
```

On this macOS host, sandboxed Chromium fails with SIGTRAP and a
MachPortRendezvousServer permission error. Rendering requires the approved
unsandboxed command. Existing voice generation is reusable; regenerate music
only when its composition or narration timing changes. Narration edits require
the workflow's draft/approval steps, then a fresh preview.

Final export writes `output/final.mp4`, `output/report.json`,
`output/automated-qc.json`, `render.snapshot.json`, and an exact archive of
shared renderer source under `src/`. Human/agent editorial evidence is in
`output/qc.json`. Sources and arithmetic are documented in `research.md`.

Guidance used: short-video workflow, discovery, script, production, visual design,
Kokoro TTS, Remotion practices, production defaults and sound QC; host environment
setup, file operations, code quality and technical documentation skills.
