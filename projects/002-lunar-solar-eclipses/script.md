## hook

One hides the Sun. The other turns the Moon red. Here's the difference.

Visual: Twin disks: Moon crosses the Sun while a second Moon darkens to copper.

## solar

In a solar eclipse, the Moon moves between the Sun and Earth. Its shadow falls on a small part of Earth.

Visual: Moon moves into Sun-Earth alignment; its narrow shadow meets Earth.
Sources: solar

## totality

From that shadow, the Moon can cover the Sun. During totality, the Sun's outer atmosphere, the corona, becomes visible.

Visual: Large Moon disk crosses the solar disk; corona appears at complete coverage.
Sources: corona

## lunar

In a lunar eclipse, Earth sits between the Sun and Moon. The Moon travels through Earth's shadow.

Visual: Earth remains central while Moon moves into its shadow cone.
Sources: lunar

## red

During a total lunar eclipse, some sunlight bends through Earth's atmosphere. More red light reaches the Moon, giving it a copper glow.

Visual: Warm light paths curve around Earth and reach a Moon that changes from gray to copper.
Sources: red

## tilt

Why not every month? The Moon's orbit is tilted about five degrees, so the shadows usually miss.

Visual: Moon follows a tilted orbital track and misses the extended shadow axis.
Sources: tilt

## recap

Solar: the Moon's shadow on Earth. Lunar: Earth's shadow on the Moon. For solar viewing, use proper eclipse glasses, never ordinary sunglasses.

Visual: Two animated alignment rows recap the shadow direction, then reveal the solar-viewing note.
