# Visual review checklist

Use this checklist on rendered frames and the actual preview. Source inspection can
find implementation risks but cannot establish visual quality.

## Contact sheet

Capture at least:

- first frame;
- end of the hook;
- midpoint of each major explanatory beat;
- proof or payoff;
- CTA/end frame.

Review the sheet as one sequence. It should look like one video with controlled
variation, not unrelated templates placed back to back.

## Story and hierarchy

- The first frame communicates the topic, tension, or result immediately.
- Every sampled frame has one obvious focal point.
- Reading order is clear without hearing the narration.
- The visual proves, demonstrates, or clarifies the current claim.
- The payoff is visually stronger or clearer than the setup.
- The CTA remains secondary to the final takeaway.

## Phone-scale legibility

- Preview at a phone-like display size, not only the full 2160×3840 default canvas.
- Headline, caption, labels, numbers, code, and chart values remain readable.
- Text does not collide with likely platform controls, title, avatar, or description.
- Caption line breaks follow phrases and normally remain within two lines.
- Text contrast remains stable on the brightest and darkest background moments.
- Key content is understandable for viewers with common color-vision differences;
  color is not the only carrier of state or meaning.

## Composition and consistency

- Alignment, margins, spacing rhythm, radii, strokes, shadows, and icon style agree.
- Accent colors retain the same semantic meaning across scenes.
- Related objects keep a predictable spatial relationship across cuts.
- Screenshots and assets are cropped to the relevant evidence and are not distorted.
- Decorative layers support the focal point rather than compete with it.
- No unintended empty frames, placeholders, watermarks, broken glyphs, or missing
  media appear.

## Motion and pacing

- Review consecutive frames or a short playback of each major action, not only
  one contact-sheet frame per scene. Save timestamps and frame sequence paths.
- Check that meaningful objects animate during the explanation; a progress bar
  or decorative pulse alone does not count as animated scene content.
- Inspect the active highlight over three consecutive spoken words and in a
  later scene/chunk. Punctuation must not flash on its own; unspoken words must
  not highlight early. Note whether timing has had an independent listening check.

- Entrances establish hierarchy instead of making all elements arrive together.
- Important copy becomes still long enough to read.
- Motion direction and easing feel related across scenes.
- Transitions express a cut, connection, comparison, or section change.
- Animation does not cover proof, crop text, cause flicker, or create accidental
  black frames.
- High-energy sequences are balanced by quieter holds before the payoff or CTA.
- Visual emphasis aligns with narration and does not fight caption, music, or SFX.

## Final gate

Block final render when any of these remain:

- unreadable or overflowing text;
- ambiguous focal point or reading order;
- caption covering proof or essential UI;
- platform control collision;
- inconsistent or misleading color meaning;
- low-resolution, distorted, missing, or unlicensed asset;
- transition or animation hiding information;
- visual claim stronger than the approved evidence;
- style drift that makes scenes feel like different videos.

Record the frames reviewed, issues found, fixes applied, and limitations that remain.
